# mechanical inheritance — collagen, prenatal movement, and the extracellular record

> The research program, scaffolded end-to-end. Collagen is not a building
> block; it is a mechanical record. The property that lets extracellular
> matrix persist through development also, in time, makes it rigid. This
> folder is the map of that program — not findings, but questions ranked by
> their evidentiary standing.

## The central thesis

> Extracellular matrix is the medium through which mechanical history
> persists in tissue. Its persistence is constructive in development,
> constraining in maturity, and limiting in age.

## The two original proposals

1. **The maternal boundary.** The prenatal environment's mechanical boundary
   is itself a hormonally remodeled maternal collagen structure (cervix,
   pelvic ligaments, uterine wall). Sexually differentiated matrix biology
   therefore enters the motile womb through the *maternal* side, where
   mammalian evidence is strong. [E1] for remodeling; [E2] for the boundary
   hypothesis.
2. **The history–plasticity axis.** Λ = H / P — recorded matrix history over
   remodeling capacity. Development is low-Λ, aging is high-Λ. "Memory" is
   purchased with plasticity: the same property that makes matrix a durable
   record also makes it rigid.
