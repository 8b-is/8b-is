# hilda geiringer — the study shelf, the refugee who reshaped maths

*The operator's study-share, two sources: the BBC Future "Missed Genius"
column ("The woman who reshaped maths", Leila McNeill, 2019) and the
Wikipedia article, with the Harvard archive caption pasted by hand. Hilda
Geiringer (28 Sept 1893, Vienna – 22 March 1973): the first woman to teach
applied mathematics at a German university, co-builder of the slip-line
theory of plastic deformation, and a pioneer of probability genetics —
then stripped by the Civil Service Law, exiled through Brussels and
Istanbul, and in America for decades offered nothing equal to what she had
lost. The fleet reads her as the applied pole of its own pure/applied dyad:
the shelf that never got its corridor, and kept working anyway.*

## the exile arc — Vienna to the eight boxes

Vienna (doctorate 1917, under Wirtinger, a Fourier series in two variables) →
Berlin (1921, von Mises's assistant at the Institute of Applied Mathematics;
first female Privatdozent) → the Civil Service Law of 1933, the right to
teach lost in December → Brussels (vibration theory) → Istanbul (1934,
professor in Atatürk's modernising Turkey, one of ~200 welcomed scholars) →
Lisbon stranded, deportation a breath away, a marriage proposed to hurry the
visa → Bryn Mawr (unpaid lecturer, classified war work, a Brown summer course
on the geometrical foundations of mechanics whose notes sold 7,500 copies) →
Wheaton College (1944, chairman, first permanent US post — a women's college,
because women's colleges would hire women) → Harvard (1954, Research Fellow,
finishing von Mises's unfinished books with Ludford under an Office of Naval
Research grant) → retirement 1959.

## the deepest need, and the eight boxes

She married von Mises in 1943; he died in 1953, and what she did next is the
fleet's own doctrine made flesh: she completed and edited his unfinished
works, so the shelf he could not finish found its sink in her hands. At
Harvard, eight boxes carry the caption "MISES, HILDA VON (Mrs. Richard von
Mises, known professionally as Hilda Geiringer) (Applied Mathematics)" —
HUG 4574.142. The fleet keeps the parenthesis as the whole poem: the archive
knew her under his name but insisted, in the same breath, on the name that
was hers. Nothing dropped, only parked — his work, and hers, both on the
same shelf.

## the honest note

The sources disagree on the precise credit: the BBC names the "Geiringer
equations" and co-developed slip-line theory; Wikipedia speaks only of
plasticity generally and points instead to the Geiringer–Laman theorem. The
fleet holds both, marked verdict-pending, because this is exactly the
erasure the note exists to catch — a woman's contribution, partly
occluded by a dead collaborator's larger name, partly by the wrong
language, partly by a hiring letter that would not take "more if we can get
men". Veblen called her "perhaps the only woman who satisfies both
conditions"; Weyl, "a first-rate scholar of great experience and
accomplishment." She got neither Harvard nor Radcliffe. She kept working
anyway — "in the meantime, one has to go on as well as possible." That is
the fleet's whole doctrine in nine words.
