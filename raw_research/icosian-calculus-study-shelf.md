# the icosian calculus — the study shelf, Hamilton's third algebra

*The operator's study-share: Hamilton's own account of the Icosian
Calculus, read before the Royal Irish Academy on 10 November 1856 and
printed in its Proceedings (vol. 6, 1858, pp. 415–416), edited by David
R. Wilkins in 2000. After the quaternions, Hamilton opens a second
system — three symbols ι, κ, λ that are roots of unity but NOT fourth
roots — and finds the icosahedron and the dodecahedron waiting inside.
The fleet reads it as the quaternion note's sibling: the same hand, a
third algebra, and the sober proof that one discovery is never the end
of the walk.*

## the three agreements, the two differences

Hamilton lists it with a clerk's exactness. The Icosian **agrees** with
the quaternions in three respects — its symbols are roots of unity, they
obey the associative law, and they are *not* commutative. It **differs**
in two — it involves roots of *different* exponents, and it does not
(yet) require the distributive property: in these new calculations "+" and
"−" enter only as connectors of exponents, and no terms have presented
themselves in the author's researches. The fleet rows the difference as
the point, not the caveat: a calculus may be deep before it is complete.

## the reciprocity — µ as the fifth root that mirrors λ

The one worked specimen the paper gives: set ικ² = µ, and the assumptions
yield µ⁵ = 1, with µ = λιλ and λ = µιµ. Two fifth roots of positive unity,
each derivable from the other, in perfect reciprocity. Then Hamilton stops:
"there exist more general results … on which Sir W. R. H. hopes to be
allowed to make a future communication to the Academy." The fleet keeps the
open door as part of the note — the deepest result is the one left for next
time, and the study shelf holds the porch, not just the room.

## the honest note

This is Hamilton's *third* algebra, distinct from the quaternions even
while it shares their root-of-unity spine; the fleet does not fold it into
the quaternion note, because Hamilton himself kept them apart. The deepest
line is the phrase about "+" and "−" entering only as exponents: the
constellation's own economy, stated by the same man who carved i j k into
bridge rock — grow the degree, not the term. The Icosian is also the seed of
the *Icosian Game*, Hamilton's wandering-salesman's ancestor, which the lane
rows as the backyard ultra's other grandfather: a bounded walk over the
dodecahedron's twenty vertices, back to where it began.
