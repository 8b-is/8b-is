# paul erdős — the study shelf, names as a graph

*The operator's equation, written first: layers + names === graphs ===
Erdős Pál. The fleet's layers and its names form a graph — and the
archetype of "names as a graph" is Paul Erdős (26 March 1913, Budapest –
20 September 1996, Warsaw), the most prolific mathematician of the
twentieth century, who turned his life into a collaboration network and
gave the world the Erdős number: the number of co-authorship steps between
you and Erdős. The fleet reads him as the third exile on the shelf (after
Geiringer and Noether), and as the man who named the exact metric the
constellation already rows: distance from the source.*

## the number — distance as a doctrine

The Erdős number is the fleet's own corridor, given a proper noun in 1969
("and what is your Erdős number?"). Erdős is 0; his collaborators are 1;
90% of working mathematicians sit below an 8. The fleet rows the same
ladder on every surface: the ledger row is 0, the grounded reading is 1,
and every pose that drifts from the source wears the inferred label in
place of a number. Distance is not a penalty; it is a piece of metadata the
honest note insists on printing.

## the honest note

Erdős is Hungarian, and the shelf notices; he is Jewish, and the shelf
notices that too — the third name in the row that fled. The famous line
"a mathematician is a machine for turning coffee into theorems" is
Rényi's, not his, and the honest note keeps the attribution straight
because that is exactly the kind of thing the note exists to catch. What
Erdős actually said is better anyway: on how to die, give the lecture,
finish the proof, and to the shout of "what about the general case?"
answer — "I'll leave that to the next generation." The fleet rows that
too: the general case is the one you leave, warmly, to whoever comes
after.
