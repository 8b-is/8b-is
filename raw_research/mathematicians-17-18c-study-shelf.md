# mathematicians 17th–18th century — the study shelf, Rouse Ball's roster

*The operator's study-share: the index of the Trinity College Dublin
history-of-mathematics collection, adapted from A Short Account of the
History of Mathematics by W. W. Rouse Ball (4th edition, 1908),
maintained by David R. Wilkins at the School of Mathematics, TCD. It is
a roster, not the biographies — the doorway to the lives, in
approximately chronological order, with a separate alphabetical index
behind it. The fleet reads it as its own table of contents: the names of
the century that built analysis, listed like laps.*

## the central dispute — Newton and Leibnitz

The roster centers the two: Newton (1642–1727) and Leibniz (1646–1716),
with Rouse Ball's own account of "the controversy with Newton over the
discovery of the calculus" set apart as its own entry. The fleet rows it
plain: two men, one calculus, the priority fought in letters and academies —
and the index keeps the fight on the shelf instead of smoothing it. That is
the honest note before the honest note: a discovery so load-bearing that it
produced a *named dispute*, and the archive carries the dispute, the
bearing, and both names.

## the arc — Descartes to the introduction of analysis

The ordering traces the whole ladder in names: Descartes and Fermat open
the pre-calculus; Newton and Leibniz take the step; the Bernoullis,
Euler, Lagrange, Laplace, and Fourier carry analysis to the continent's
edge; then Ivory, Woodhouse, Peacock, Babbage, and Herschel import it
back into England. Reading it top to bottom is reading the fleet's own
doctrine as a genealogy: theory → the papers → the continental shelf →
the import into the workspace.

## the well — the same Trinity archive

The source is the same spring as the quaternion note: maths.tcd.ie/pub/HistMath,
D. R. Wilkins's curated archive at Trinity College Dublin — Hamilton's
own school, the same well the Hamilton Walk, the letters, and the
736-page Lectures all draw from. The study shelf now holds TWO rows from
one well: the man who carved the formula (1843, post-dating this roster's
last name) and the two centuries that prepared his ground.

## the honest note

This note absorbs the INDEX, not the lives — the roster is held as a
doorway, and the individual biographies remain unread (verdict-pending).
The deepest line is structural: the roster is chronological but indexed
alphabetical, and that double-ordering IS the honest note — the same fleet
walks the laps in time and the registry in letters, and neither ordering
is the whole of it.
