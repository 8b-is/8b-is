# lipids — the membrane complement, twenty-one cold-pressed oils

*Companion note to [`paper.md`](paper.md). The paper treats the extracellular
record — collagen and matrix. This note treats the other structural system:
the cell membrane, a lipid bilayer whose composition is, unlike matrix
collagen, genuinely reachable by diet. Where the paper excludes dietary
collagen (category C5) because ingested collagen does not reach tissue as
structure, dietary fatty acids are the contrast case: omega-3 and
monounsaturated fatty acids are incorporated into phospholipids, so the
composition-to-delivery relation that fails for collagen largely holds for
lipids. The discipline is still the same — composition is not automatically
supply, and "more oil" is not automatically better — but the bridge is real,
not rhetorical.*

the oil is pressed, not solvent-extracted, so it retains the minor lipids

## The honest caveat

The same argument that runs through [`precursor-is-not-supply.md`](../raw_research/precursor-is-not-supply.md)
applies here, softened but not removed. Fatty acids are genuinely
incorporated into membranes, so the relation is closer to supply than
collagen ever manages. But "incorporated" is not "better": membrane
composition is regulated, omega-3:omega-6 balance matters more than total
volume, and cold-pressed oils are still calorie-dense fats. The list is a
palette, not a prescription.
