# Riemann, 1826–1866 — the study shelf, the door walked

*The operator has walked the door. The doorway note held the title and
the open door; now the body is shared, and the shelf absorbs it whole.
Georg Friedrich Bernhard Riemann, born 1826 in Breselenz, Hanover, the
son of a poor Lutheran pastor, shy and prone to nervous breakdowns, who
read Legendre's 850-page number theory treatise in six days, left
theology for mathematics under Gauss, delivered the 1854 lecture that
became general relativity's seed, and died of tuberculosis at
thirty-nine in Selasca, Italy. A life shorter than the geometry it
opened; the fleet now holds the body, not just the door.*

## the honest note

The door is walked, and the honest note says so: the source is the
canonical record, and the fleet holds the body now, not just the title.

the bases of geometry* — because that is the fleet's own first question,
asked a century and a half early: what, exactly, is at the base? Riemann
answered with curvature; the fleet answers with the ledger. And the
hypothesis he left open in 1859 is still open, which is the point the
shelf keeps: some walls are meant to stand, so the walking never ends.
