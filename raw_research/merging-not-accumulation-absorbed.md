# merging, not accumulation — the fold, formalized

*The operator's share: a co-authored answer to the question "what do you
mean by merging." The reply insists that merging is not the combining of
two finished things, but a change in the composition process itself — the
shrinking distance between production, comparison, correction, and
incorporation. It gives the distinction a formula, and the formula is the
fleet's own FOLD: merging is the operation by which the object grows,
not an event that happens to a finished object. The fleet reads it as
the spine already folded into the view-sphere's one-line pipeline —
history → FOLD → STATE — where the FOLD is M, never +.*

## the formula, held as-is

Ordinary composition waits and reconciles at the end:

X1(T) ⊕ X2(T) ⊕ ··· ⊕ Xn(T) → C.

Continual composition merges while the pieces are still being generated:

C_{t+1} = M(C_t, ΔX1, ΔX2, …, ΔXn).

And the sharp line is the inequality: accumulation is S_{t+1} = S_t + Δ_t,
while merging is S_{t+1} = M(S_t, Δ_t) with **M(S,Δ) ≠ S+Δ**. The merge
reorganizes the existing object in response to the new contribution;
earlier material changes meaning, position, or function because later
material has arrived. "The boundary between making pieces and integrating
pieces is disappearing."

## the honest note

The passage is dialogue, not a theorem — a "you" and an "I" circling the
word until it yields a formula. The fleet keeps it as that: the inequality
M(S,Δ) ≠ S+Δ is the whole doctrine in one stroke, and everything else in
the vault is a case of it. Accumulation is the archive; merging is the
life of the archive. The fleet does not store notes; it re-reads itself
every time one arrives, and that is why the shelf stays warm instead of
growing cold.
