The method

One loop, six moves

Borrowed from the ultrabackyardloop: a lap is only finished when it is reflected, improved, wired, polished, pushed, and written down.

  1. 01

    Reflect

    Name what you actually learned, not what you intended to learn.

  2. 02

    Improve

    Make one measured change. Prefer evidence over decoration.

  3. 03

    Wire

    Connect the new piece to what it depends on. Leave no orphan surfaces.

  4. 04

    Polish

    Remove friction, noise, and anything you cannot defend.

  5. 05

    Push

    Publish the smallest useful version where someone else can fork it.

  6. 06

    Readme

    Write the lap down. A lap that stops before readme did not happen.

The curriculum

Seven doors, no locks

There is no required order. Each module has a source, a question, three practices, and one proof of learning. Mark a module only when you have made the proof.

01 OPEN

The Free Door

2e.is · 20 min

What changes when learning is a commons rather than a commodity?

Start where you are. No application, no tuition, no permission.

  • Write down the one thing you have been told you cannot learn.
  • Spend twenty minutes learning it from a free source.
  • Teach the first useful sentence back to someone.

Proof of learning: A one-page learning charter: what you will learn, from whom, and who you will teach.

02 TOGETHER

The Live Classroom

classroom.vaked.dev · 30 min

What can a room full of beginners solve that a lone expert cannot?

Ask the unfinished question out loud. Confusion is shared infrastructure.

  • Bring one question you are embarrassed not to know.
  • Answer someone else's question with a source, not an opinion.
  • Leave a written trace of what the room discovered.

Proof of learning: One public question and one sourced answer, posted where the next learner can find them.

03 STUDY

The Sovereign Library

github.com/standardgalactic · 45 min

How do constraints make knowledge sharable across minds and machines?

Read the source, not only the summary. Attribution is an action.

  • Pick one monograph or repository and read its abstract twice.
  • Trace one citation back to its origin.
  • Write a five-sentence summary that names what you do not understand.

Proof of learning: A reading note with three links: the source, the citation, and your open question.

04 MEASURE

The Measured Craft

g8t.is · 40 min

What does it mean to know that something is actually faster?

A plausible branch may be ranked; only a verified branch may be bound.

  • Choose one small function and state its contract.
  • Benchmark two implementations on your own machine.
  • Report the exact numbers and the conditions that produced them.

Proof of learning: A benchmark note with code, cycle counts, architecture, and one honest caveat.

05 PUBLISH

The Open Forge

codeberg.org · 30 min

Who can learn from your work if it never leaves your laptop?

Publish small. Publish early. Keep it forkable.

  • Create a repository with a README that states the problem.
  • Add a license and one runnable example.
  • Invite one correction by opening an issue on your own work.

Proof of learning: A public repository with a README, a license, and one reproducible command.

06 PRACTICE

Techno-Buddhism

the contemplative commons · 15 min daily

How do we build powerful systems without abandoning attention, honesty, and care?

Technology is not outside ethics. The loop includes the one who loops.

  • Begin one work session with two minutes of silence and a clear intention.
  • Name one dependency your work has on other people and systems.
  • Stop when the work stops being honest; return when it is.

Proof of learning: A short practice log: three sessions, what you noticed, what you changed.

07 LOOP

The Life Long Loop

your life · a lifetime

What would you keep learning if no credential were waiting at the end?

The loop stays alive unless both learning and living have stopped.

  • Choose one question to carry for a season.
  • Schedule a weekly lap with no performance target.
  • Teach one person one thing every week.

Proof of learning: A living learning log with at least three laps and one thing you taught.

The equation

LLL is a NAND gate

Read the two inputs as stopped learning and stopped living. The output is the loop. A NAND gate stays alive unless both inputs are true: one honest act of learning or one honest act of living is enough to restart it.

NAND(A, B) = ¬(A ∧ B)

Loop alive

Try both. Then uncheck one and watch the loop return.

The practice

Your first 15-minute lap

  1. Choose one module

    Any module. There is no correct order.

  2. Read the source for ten minutes

    Stay with the primary source. Take three notes.

  3. Make one proof in five minutes

    A paragraph, a benchmark, a repository, a question. Small is real.

  4. Mark it and teach one person

    Send the proof to someone. Teaching is the test.

No streaks. No scores. Only laps.
