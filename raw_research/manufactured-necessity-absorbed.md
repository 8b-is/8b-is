# manufactured necessity, absorbed — Flyxion's counterfactual of the capital stock

*A new Flyxion paper (September 2026): environmental accounting of large
discretionary infrastructure (cruise ships, destination resorts) is
almost always MARGINAL — per-user intensity inside a fixed capital
stock. The prior question — should the capital stock exist at all —
requires a STRUCTURAL accounting: the counterfactual burden of the
artifact against a world where it was never built. The paper isolates
the regime transition (marginal → structural) and shows operational
efficiency can never close the gap between the two when the
infrastructure's existence is the decision under dispute.*

## the lane's mapping

| the paper's term | the constellation's shape |
|---|---|
| marginal waste — burden per added user in a fixed stock | per-run efficiency: better losses, faster laps, lower token burn inside the lanes that exist |
| structural waste — the burden of the stock's existence | the counterfactual of the whole fleet: what did building the constellation cost, against a world where it wasn't built |
| the regime transition, driven by demand persistence = the asset's own reinvestment cycle | the stream/lap lifecycle: a sustained need that outlives a single bake moves the evaluation from "run it better" to "should it exist" |
| opportunity cost of foreclosed alternatives (land, capital, labor) | the sand: every U.2 terabyte, every GPU hour, every M1 cycle is a foreclosed alternative use |
| "operational gains cannot close the gap between marginal and structural" | efficiency is the castle's polish; existence is the castle's warrant — no amount of polishing answers whether to build |
