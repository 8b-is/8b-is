# research — the frontier, the open questions, the lane's contribution

*The third pillar. The base named the mechanism, the foundation named the
fix. This pillar names what is still open, and what the fleet can actually
do about it. It is where the lane's doctrine — show me the mechanism, the
ledger rows everything, the corridor stays open — meets a real, live problem.*

## 1. The open questions

### (a) Biomarkers — we cannot measure the fix well enough

The current readouts (CK, muscle MRI, strength scores) lag the disease and
lag the repair. To run trials fast and to tell whether a therapy works
before the years pass, the field needs **fluid and imaging biomarkers** that
track muscle health in near-real time. This is the *measurement* problem —
the lane's own "z does not determine its measurement."

### (b) Natural history — the baseline is still thin

Because each subtype is rare, the natural-history data (how fast each one
progresses, untreated) is sparse. Without a known baseline, a trial cannot
separate "slowed" from "naturally slow." This is the *endpoint fallacy* made
clinical: the destination is recorded, the trajectory is not.

### (c) Delivery & immune management — the walls from the foundation pillar

Systemic delivery to every fiber plus heart and diaphragm; pre-existing
anti-AAV antibodies; redosing; the dysferlin size ceiling. These are
engineering problems with active, partially-answered solutions.

### (d) Combinatorial therapy — one fix is rarely enough

For the glycosylation defect, gene therapy *and* substrate together may beat
either alone. For the structural defect, a functional copy plus membrane
stabilization. The frontier is **combinations**, and combinations need the
biomarkers from (a) to be judged.

### (e) The dominant forms — the unsolved corner

Gain-of-function and toxic dominant alleles resist replacement. Suppression
(RNAi, ASO knockdown, gene editing) is harder and less mature. This is the
least-served corner of the family.

## 2. The lane's contribution — what the fleet actually does

The fleet is not a clinic and not a lab; it is a **mapping and ledger
engine**. What it can genuinely contribute:

1. **The honest map** — this folder. The mechanism taxonomy (five classes,
   four modalities) turns a 32-subtype tangle into a decision tool: name the
   gene, and the fix is implied.
2. **The trial registry (v2)** — a machine-readable table of every active
   LGMD trial (sponsor, phase, vector, endpoint, NCT), kept current. The
   ledger's natural shape: a row per trial, purged of nothing, open to query.
3. **The biomarker decision map (v2)** — which readout answers which question,
   for each subtype. The lane's "show me the mechanism" applied to measurement.
4. **The corridor** — publication here, in the open, so the map is forkable
   and the next researcher starts where this one left off. Readability is
   freedom.

## 3. The research's one rule

**The map is the contribution.** The fleet cannot administer a therapy, but
it can make the mechanism legible, the trials queryable, and the open
questions explicit — and legibility is the thing that accelerates every
other actor in the field.

## 4. Honest note

No shelf in this folder is a treatment, and none is medical advice. For any
real case the first, non-negotiable move is a neuromuscular clinic and a
genetic diagnosis. What this folder is: the base, the foundation, and the
research laid out honestly, versioned, and pushed to the org — so that the
"try" the operator keeps saying is pointed at the right wall.
